1. Setup: current bid/ask and volumes
Suppose the market for a stock looks like this:

Bid price: 
$9.90

Ask price: 
$10.00

Bid volume: 
𝑉bid=5,000 shares

Ask volume: 
𝑉ask=12,000 shares

From a trader’s point of view:

You can sell immediately at 
$9.90 (hit the bid).

You can buy immediately at 
$10.00 (lift the ask).

2. Compute Bid–Ask Volume Ratio
We define:

𝑅=𝑉ask𝑉bid
Plug in the numbers:

𝑅=12,0005,000=2.4
Interpretation:

𝑅=2.4
>1.3 → strong buying pressure

Ask‑side aggression (buyers lifting the ask) dominates bid‑side aggression.

3. What this means for price
If traders keep buying at the ask:

The 12,000 shares at 
$10.00 get consumed.

Next available ask might be 
$10.05 or $10.10
.Price ticks up as buyers chase higher offers.

So with:
𝑅=2.4you’d say:

“Ask volume is much larger than bid volume → buyers are more aggressive → probability of price increase is high.”

4. Bearish example (reverse situation)
Now flip the numbers:

Bid volume: 
𝑉bid=15,000

Ask volume: 
𝑉ask=4,000

Then:

𝑅=𝑉ask𝑉bid=4,00015,000≈0.27
Interpretation:

𝑅=0.27<0.77 → strong selling pressure

Bid‑side aggression (sellers hitting the bid) dominates.

If traders keep selling at the bid:

The 15,000 shares at 
$9.90 get consumed.

Next available bid might be 
$9.80 or $9.75
.
Price ticks down as sellers accept lower bids.

So here you’d say:

“Bid volume is much larger than ask volume → sellers are more aggressive → probability of price decrease is high.”

5. Summary in one formula
Bullish pressure:

𝑅=𝑉ask𝑉bid>1.3⇒price more likely to rise
Bearish pressure:

𝑅=𝑉ask𝑉bid<0.77⇒price more likely to fall
